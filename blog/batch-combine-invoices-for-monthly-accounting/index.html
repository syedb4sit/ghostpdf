Monthly Bookkeeping: Combine Receipt Images and Invoices into One PDF

Consolidate mixed photo receipts and PDF vendor invoices into a numbered monthly expense packet for your accountant.

1. Why Accountants Reject Mixed Receipt Folders (and How One Packet Fixes It)

At month-end close, bookkeepers routinely receive twenty separate downloads: smartphone JPEG photos of thermal fuel receipts, PNG screenshots of SaaS billing portals, and native PDF invoices from vendors. Reviewing disparate files forces tax preparers to open dozens of windows, manually cross-check totals, and chase missing pages.

Consolidating image captures into uniform PDF pages first—and merging them chronologically behind a unified bank statement or cover summary—turns fragmented expense folders into a single indexed ledger ready for audit software and cloud ERP import.

2. Step-by-Step Monthly Expense Packet Assembly

  1. Convert Photo Receipts First: Upload camera JPGs and portal PNG screenshots into Image to PDF, ordering each receipt chronologically into one clean PDF batch.
  2. Combine With Native Vendor PDFs: Open Merge PDF and drag your cover sheet, converted receipt batch, and digital vendor PDF invoices into date sequence.
  3. Stamp Consecutive Audit Numbers: Run the merged packet through Page Numbers so every receipt bears an unambiguous Page X of N footer.
  4. Compress Before Emailing Your CPA: If smartphone photos pushed the packet above 25 MB, run Medium Compression so it clears corporate email attachment filters cleanly.

4. Bookkeeping Quality Checks Before Closing the Month

  • Rotate Sideways Phone Captures Immediately: Mobile camera EXIF rotation sometimes leaves tall receipts sideways. Fix orientation in Rotate PDF before merging with portrait invoices.
  • Scrub Personal Identifiers When Filing Grant Audits: If sharing expense packets with external auditors or third-party boards, black out private card numbers cleanly via Redact PDF.
  • Verify Readable Cents & Tax Lines: After running compression on blurry thermal paper photos, zoom to 150% on one receipt to verify GST/VAT totals stayed crisp.
