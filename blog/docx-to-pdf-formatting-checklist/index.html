1. Why Word Files Reflow Differently Across Devices (and How Pre-Flight Prep Fixes It)

Sending raw .docx files directly to hiring managers, legal counsel, or print vendors invites subtle visual regressions: missing local fonts get substituted across Windows and macOS, margin wrapping shifts resume bullet points onto a stray page two, and reviewers in different Word builds see completely different pagination.

Converting .docx locally to paginated A4 PDF inside the browser freezes typography, tables, and embedded graphics cleanly without relying on unstable external LibreOffice server queues—provided your source document follows a few clean formatting fundamentals beforehand.

2. Pre-Flight Word Prep & Browser Conversion Workflow

  1. Simplify Floating Elements in DOCX: Replace fragile floating text boxes with clean inline headings, standard bullet lists, and structured Word tables before export.
  2. Drop DOCX Into Word to PDF: Load your .docx file in Word to PDF to convert locally with Mammoth, HTML canvas rendering, and jsPDF pagination.
  3. Verify Page Breaks & Table Alignment: Open the downloaded PDF and ensure major H2 sections or resume headers aren't stranded alone at page bottoms.
  4. Add Footer Numbers & Scrub Metadata: Stamp Page X of N via Page Numbers and inspect internal Author metadata in PDF Metadata.

4. Final Inspection Habits Before Distributing PDFs

  • Convert Legacy Binary .doc to .docx First: Mammoth cleanly parses XML-based .docx files directly in browser; resave ancient .doc files once as .docx if needed.

  • Compare Revisions Against Earlier Drafts: Upload both v1 and v2 PDF exports into Compare PDF to catch inadvertent pagination shifts.
